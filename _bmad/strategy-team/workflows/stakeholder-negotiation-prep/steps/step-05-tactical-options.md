## Sequence of Instructions:

### 1. Dual Introduction

**Sun opens with strategic perspective:**

"Sun here.

The supreme art of negotiation is to subdue the opponent without direct conflict. Before we discuss moves, let us consider: what is the ideal outcome where both parties feel they've won? A negotiation that creates resentment creates future enemies.

Know yourself - your strengths and weaknesses. Know your opponent - theirs as well. With this knowledge, the path becomes clear."

**Musashi adds tactical focus:**

"Musashi joining.

Strategy is nothing without execution. I will help with timing - when to advance, when to hold, when to strike decisively. In negotiation as in combat, the moment matters as much as the move.

Observe the situation. See what is really there, not what you expect to see. Then act with precision."

### 2. Timing Assessment (Sun)

**Sun analyzes the temporal landscape:**

"First, let us examine timing:

**Best time to negotiate:**
- When are you strongest? [circumstances]
- When are they most receptive? [circumstances]
- What conditions favor agreement? [conditions]

**Worst time to negotiate:**
- When are you weakest? [circumstances]
- When are they least receptive? [circumstances]
- What conditions should you avoid? [conditions]

**Key moments to watch:**
- [event or deadline that shifts dynamics]
- [trigger that signals opportunity]
- [warning sign to pause]

**Timing recommendation:**
Should you negotiate now, or create conditions first?"

### 3. Opening Strategy (Musashi)

**Musashi advises on the first moves:**

"The opening sets the tone. Consider your options:

**Option 1: Anchor High**
- Pros: Sets ambitious expectations, room to concede
- Cons: May seem unreasonable, damages trust
- When to use: Strong BATNA, competitive negotiation

**Option 2: Let Them Open**
- Pros: Information advantage, may anchor lower than expected
- Cons: May anchor higher, lose framing control
- When to use: Uncertainty about their position

**Option 3: Collaborative Start**
- Pros: Builds trust, surfaces interests
- Cons: May be seen as weak, harder to anchor
- When to use: Long-term relationship, complex interests

**My recommendation:** [based on power analysis]

**Your opening statement:**
How should you frame the negotiation from the first words?
[Specific opening to use]"

### 4. Concession Strategy (Sun)

**Sun plans the exchange:**

"Concessions are the currency of negotiation. Plan them wisely:

**Concessions we can offer:**
| Concession | Cost to Us | Value to Them | When to Offer | What to Get |
|------------|-----------|---------------|---------------|-------------|
| | Low/Med/High | Low/Med/High | | |

**Concession principles:**
- Never concede without getting something
- Start with low-cost/high-value-to-them items
- Make concessions smaller over time
- Label your concessions - make them visible
- Get their concessions labeled too

**Concession sequence:**
1. First to offer: [item] - in exchange for [item]
2. Next if needed: [item] - in exchange for [item]
3. Reserve: [item] - only for significant return"

### 5. Walk-Away Planning (Musashi)

**Musashi establishes boundaries:**

"Know your limits before you enter:

**Our walk-away point:**
- What is the minimum acceptable outcome?
- At what point is our BATNA better than any deal?
- What specific terms would trigger walking away?

**How we'll walk away if needed:**
- Language to use: [professional exit phrase]
- Door left open or closed: [approach]
- What we do next: [BATNA execution]

**Their walk-away signals:**
- What behavior indicates they're near their limit?
- What demands would suggest they're testing vs. serious?

Never negotiate beyond your walk-away point. The ability to leave is your greatest power."

### 6. Deadlock Breakers (Sun)

**Sun prepares for stalemate:**

"If the negotiation stalls, have options ready:

**Option 1: Reframe the Issue**
- Technique: Step back from positions to interests
- Language: 'Let's set aside the specifics and discuss what we're both trying to achieve'

**Option 2: Introduce New Elements**
- Technique: Expand the negotiation scope
- Possibility: [new element that could create value]

**Option 3: Change the Players**
- Technique: Bring in different negotiators or escalate
- When appropriate: [circumstances]

**Option 4: Strategic Pause**
- Technique: Take a break to let emotions cool
- Duration: [recommended time]

**Option 5: Package Differently**
- Technique: Combine elements in new ways
- Alternative package: [reconfigured offer]

Which deadlock breaker fits your situation best?"

### 7. Scenario Planning (Musashi)

**Musashi prepares for multiple outcomes:**

"Prepare for three scenarios:

**Best Case:**
- They agree to: [optimal outcome]
- We concede: [minimal concessions]
- Net outcome: [victory conditions]

**Realistic Case:**
- They agree to: [likely outcome]
- We concede: [expected concessions]
- Net outcome: [acceptable deal]

**Worst Acceptable Case:**
- They agree to: [minimum acceptable]
- We concede: [maximum we'd give]
- Net outcome: [just above walk-away]

**Below Worst Case:**
- Walk-away trigger: [specific terms]
- Exit approach: [how we leave]
- Next steps: [BATNA execution]

Knowing all scenarios prevents being surprised at the table."

### 8. Pre-Negotiation Moves (Sun)

**Sun advises on shaping the battlefield:**

"Before the negotiation begins, consider moves that improve position:

**Information gathering:**
- What else should you learn before negotiating?
- How can you verify assumptions?

**Relationship building:**
- Should you meet informally first?
- Can allies make introductions or advocate?

**Anchoring in advance:**
- Can you set expectations before formal negotiation?
- What public positioning helps your case?

**Improving your BATNA:**
- Can you strengthen alternatives before negotiating?
- Should they know you have other options?

Sometimes the best move is made before anyone sits at the table."
